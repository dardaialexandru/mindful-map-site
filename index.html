What the map is made of

Fourteen needs: seven for the body, seven for the mind. A feeling is a signal that one of them may not be met. That link is our working idea, not a result of studies.

The body needs
The trunk of the tree
  • SleepShown to help · solid evidenceEnough, and at regular hours.
  • MovementSome evidence · effect small or mixedWalking, strength, anything that raises your pulse.
  • FoodSome evidence · effect small or mixedWhat and how you eat.
  • Rhythm and lightSome evidence · effect small or mixedRegular days and daylight, especially in the morning.
  • TouchSome evidence · effect small or mixedWanted, and often.
  • A safe placeSome evidence · effect small or mixedClean air, quiet, green space, somewhere you can rest.
  • Intimacy and reproductionNot enough evidence yetCloseness, sex, and children for those who want them.
The mind needs
The crown of the tree
  • ControlSome evidence · effect small or mixedBeing able to decide about your own life.
  • CompetenceSome evidence · effect small or mixedFeeling capable.
  • BelongingSome evidence · effect small or mixedPeople you feel close to.
  • MeaningSome evidence · effect small or mixedFeeling that life has a point.
  • StabilitySome evidence · effect small or mixedMoney, work and a place to live.
  • RecognitionObserved link · cause not shownBeing seen and fairly rewarded.
  • Creativity and imaginationSome evidence · older adults, in groupsMaking things, with other people.
* Every perspective on this page is flexible and relative.

What helps one person may not help another. You choose where you place your energy. A separate space for the soul and spiritual concepts is coming soon.

The branches and the emotions

Two more parts of the map. The branches are the routes between body and mind. Emotions are neither body nor mind alone, and they come pleasant and unpleasant.

Mind and body
The branches of the tree
  • BreathingSome evidence · small trials, moderate risk of biasSlow, steady breathing is one of the few things that link body and mind in both directions.
  • RelaxationSome evidence · medium effect on anxietyLetting the muscles and the mind loosen, on purpose.
  • Stress and the body's defencesSolid link in studies · illness effect not shownShort stress prepares the body. Stress that never ends wears it down.
  • Inflammation and moodSome evidence · applies to some peopleFor some people, a body in low-grade inflammation and a low mood travel together.
  • Hormones and signalsSome evidence · small gaps, easily over-soldHormones carry messages between body and mind. Some of the popular stories about them do not hold up.
  • Heart rhythm and calmObserved link · cause not shownThe beat-to-beat rhythm of your heart carries a message about how settled your body is.
  • Gut and mindEarly evidence · few small studiesThe gut and the brain talk to each other. For mood, the evidence is still early.
  • How you handle feelingsSolid link · mostly from short testsWhat you do with a feeling matters about as much as the feeling itself.

Emotions
Pleasant and unpleasant
  • CalmSome evidence · ways to reach it are testedThe settled, unhurried state. Not an absence of feeling, but a body at ease.
  • AweEarly evidence · measured in short experimentsThe feeling of standing in front of something much bigger than you.
  • Joy and good feelingsObserved link · cause not shownFeeling well, cheerful or glad, and whether it matters for the body.
  • AngerObserved link · mostly explained by habitsThe heat that rises when something feels unfair or blocked.
  • GriefSome evidence · helps those who struggle mostWhat losing someone, or something, does to a person.

How sure is each point?

Every page says how sure we are. The line shows it, and the words say it too.

Shown to help · solid evidenceShown to help

Several good studies with a comparison group point the same way. The effect is usually small or medium.

Some evidence · effect small or mixedSome evidence

The direction is clear, but the effect is small, mixed, or the studies are weak.

Not enough evidence yetNot enough evidence

People with one thing tend to have the other, or there is too little research. We say what we do not know.

Popular ideas that did not hold up when tested again are left off the map, for example habits forming in 21 days, willpower running out like a battery, and “power poses”. More on how we choose what goes on the map.
