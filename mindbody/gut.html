Mind and body

Gut and mind

The gut and the brain talk to each other. For mood, the evidence is still early.

Early evidence · few small studies

In plain words

Trillions of bacteria live in your gut, and the gut is wired to the brain. Scientists are asking whether they affect mood. So far the answer is a cautious maybe.

What research says

A review of 16 studies found that people with depression had fewer of some gut bacteria (including Faecalibacterium) than people without. This came from 10 studies with 701 people.

In 6 trials with 302 people, probiotics reduced depression symptoms more than control treatments. The range around the result was very wide.

How far to trust itFew and small studies. Diet and medicines were not well controlled, so they could explain the bacteria differences. The probiotic trials differ in strain, dose and who took part. This is not yet a treatment.

What you can try

  • Eating a mix of plants and fibre is good for the gut and has other benefits. The Food page has more.
  • Probiotics are not a replacement for treatment of depression.
  • If your gut and your mood both trouble you, tell a doctor about both.
