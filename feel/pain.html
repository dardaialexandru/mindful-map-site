Feeling

Pain

Aching, sore, or hurting for a while. These needs may be linked to it. Start at the top, once a doctor has looked at it.

See a doctor firstGet new, strong or unexplained pain checked. Call 112 for chest pain, sudden severe pain, or pain with numbness, weakness, fever or trouble breathing. This page is about ongoing pain that a doctor has already looked at.

  1. Movement

    Exercise often eased long-lasting pain in an overview of 21 reviews. The certainty is low, and the main side effect was sore muscles.

    Some evidence · effect small or mixed

  2. Sleep

    Short or broken nights made pain feel stronger in lab studies of healthy people. For people who already have long-lasting pain, the evidence is thin.

    Some evidence · effect small or mixed

  3. Touch

    Massage helped in a few kinds of pain, such as long-lasting low back pain and fibromyalgia. For most others the certainty was low.

    Some evidence · effect small or mixed

  4. Competence

    Learning ways to cope, such as cognitive behavioural therapy, gave small pain relief in 75 studies of about 9,400 people. The certainty was moderate.

    Some evidence · effect small or mixed

How these fit together

Pain is not only in the body. Short nights can make it feel louder, and gentle movement and a feeling that you can manage it often go together. Start with whichever stop is easiest today, and keep your doctor in the loop.

In everyday life

Made-up examples to help you see yourself. They are not study results.

Elena, 52 has a sore lower back most days and was afraid to move. A doctor checked it first. Now she tries ten-minute walks, and movement is the first stop on her list.
Tom, 38 wakes at 3 am with a stiff neck, and everything hurts more the next day. Sleep sits near the top of his list.
