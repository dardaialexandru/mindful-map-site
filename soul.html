Ideas about the soul and inner life, from different traditions and from personal experience.
What it is not

Where it will live
Under the tree, but kept apart from the body and mind pages, so neither weakens the other.

What the picture holds. A lotus, a flame, a tree of life with branches above and roots below, spirals, and rings like a mandala. Each is shared by many traditions and belongs to none alone. We left out the sacred marks of single faiths on purpose, so no one is made to feel it is theirs or not theirs.

Why the soul sits apart

The tree holds what research can test: sleep, movement, people, purpose. The soul is a different kind of question. It belongs to tradition, belief and personal experience, and people reach very different answers in good faith. We would rather give it its own space than mix it in.

Some ideas people have held

These are short, simplified descriptions, not endorsements.

  • Ancient Greece. The word for soul, psyche, began as the breath of life. Philosophers such as Plato described it as the part of us that thinks and moves us.
  • Hindu thought. The self, atman, is the lasting core of a person.
  • Buddhist teaching. There is no fixed self. What we call a self is a changing flow of experience.
  • Judaism, Christianity and Islam. Each speaks of a soul given by God. They differ on what it is and what comes after.
  • Many Indigenous traditions. Life and spirit run through the land, animals and ancestors, and not only through one person.
  • Without religion. Some people use the word for their deepest values, or for what makes them feel most alive.
