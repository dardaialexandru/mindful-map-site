Mind need

Belonging

People you feel close to.

Strong link in many studies · cause not proven

In plain words

Belonging is having people who know you and care how you are. The studies found that a few warm relationships mattered more than many distant ones. It is about quality, not how many friends you have.

A simple way to picture it. The research sections below say how sure we are.

What research says

In a review of 148 studies with 308,849 people followed for about seven and a half years, those with stronger relationships tended to live longer and stay healthier. The difference was about 50% better odds.

The quality of relationships mattered more than their number.

A famous Harvard study of adult life found that satisfaction with relationships at 50 predicted health later better than cholesterol did.

How far to trust itThese studies observe people, so they cannot prove that relationships protect health. Results differ a lot between studies. The Harvard group is small and not very varied, and we know it only from press reports. We could not read the studies on how to move out of loneliness, so we give no advice on that yet.

What you can try

  • Look after a few relationships that feel warm.
  • If loneliness lasts for weeks, talk to a doctor or a counsellor.

In everyday life

Made-up examples to help you see yourself. They are not study results.

Teo, 31 moved to a new city and knows 300 people on his phone but would call none of them. One neighbour who asks how he is does more for him than all of that.
Mara, 57 phones her sister every Sunday. The call is not long, but it is the one fixed point of her week.
Ben, 22 is shy and avoids groups. Starting with one person, one coffee, is a smaller step than a party.

Each line joins this need to another. The label on it says how well the link is backed by research, and how many studies the page text names. Tap a name to open it. A link seen in studies is not proof of cause.

  • FoodSeen in studies
    In 41 reviews about children and teenagers, more family meals went with less depression and better family relations. The studies cannot show cause, and we do not know whether this holds for adults.
  • TouchThin evidence
    A narrative review of touch and loneliness (2022) argues that the two interact: whether and what kind of touch you have may shape how lonely you feel. It is a discussion, not a count of studies, so the evidence is thin.
  • Intimacy and reproductionSeen in studies
    A review of 228 records with 248,021 people found that better sexual satisfaction and function went with higher relationship satisfaction and less psychological distress. Almost all of it (96%) looked at one moment in time, so cause is not shown.
  • MeaningSeen in studies
    In a pooling of 135,227 adults from 36 cohorts, people with a stronger sense of purpose felt less lonely, and people without purpose were more likely to become lonely later. It is observational, so cause is not proven.
  • CompetenceSame framework
    Control, competence and belonging are the three basic needs studied together in the large review of 881 groups of people.
