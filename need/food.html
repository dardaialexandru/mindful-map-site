Body need

Food

What and how you eat.

Observed link · a cause is not shown

In plain words

Food is more than fuel. What and how we eat shapes our days: our energy, our sleep, even who we sit down with. The studies so far show a pattern, not a rule. People who eat a lot of ultra-processed food tend to feel worse, but they also differ from other people in other ways.

A simple way to picture it. The research sections below say how sure we are.

What research says

A review of 45 reviews, covering about 10 million people, found that people who eat a lot of ultra-processed food tend to have worse mood and more anxiety, and poorer health in general.

These are links between two things. They do not prove that the food is the cause, because people who eat this way also differ in other ways.

Trials that tested diet as a way to lift mood are few and weak. A Mediterranean diet showed a medium effect in 5 trials, but confidence is low and a new study could find no effect.

How far to trust itStudies measure ultra-processed food in different ways, and many other factors were not controlled. We do not have good evidence that changing your diet changes your mood.

What you can try

  • Eating fewer ultra-processed foods is a reasonable direction.
  • Do not expect food alone to change your mood.
  • If your mood stays low for weeks, talk to a doctor.

In everyday life

Made-up examples to help you see yourself. They are not study results.

Sofia, 31 skips breakfast, eats a vending-machine lunch and a late takeaway. She is not sure food is the problem. The page agrees it is hard to know, and suggests only a gentle direction: a bit less ultra-processed food.
Karim, 45 has changed his diet three times this year, hoping it would lift his mood. The evidence does not promise that food alone will, so he is free to enjoy eating without turning it into a test.
Ioana, 36 eats dinner with her family most evenings. The studies here do not measure that, but it is a reminder that meals are also about people.
