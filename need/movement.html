Body need

Movement

Walking, strength, anything that raises your pulse.

Some evidence · small studies, low certainty

In plain words

Movement does not have to mean sport. Anything that raises your pulse a little counts, from a brisk walk to lifting something heavy. The review found that the kind mattered less than finding one you will actually keep doing.

A simple way to picture it. The research sections below say how sure we are.

What research says

In a review of 218 studies with 14,170 people, every kind of exercise lifted mood compared with doing nothing. The difference was medium to large.

Walking or jogging, yoga and strength training worked about as well as each other. Strength training and yoga had fewer people dropping out. Light and hard effort both helped, and exercise added to medication helped too.

Sitting a lot goes with poorer health, but only among the least active people. In a review of 9 studies with 850,060 people, the most active showed no extra risk.

How far to trust itMost of these studies were small, and the review rates its certainty as low for walking and jogging and very low for the rest. Exercise probably lifts mood. We cannot say by how much.

What you can try

  • Pick a kind you can keep doing. Walking counts.
  • If you dropped out of exercise before, try strength training or yoga.
  • If you sit most of the day, short bursts of moving still matter.

In everyday life

Made-up examples to help you see yourself. They are not study results.

Dan, 52 stopped going to the gym years ago because he hated the machines. He walks the dog for 30 minutes after dinner. It is not a plan, but it is regular, and regular is the part that lasts.
Priya, 27 sits at a desk for nine hours. She sets a reminder to stand and walk to the window every hour. Short bursts still matter, as the page says.
Tom, 38 tried running three times and quit each time. Yoga and strength training had fewer people dropping out in the review, so he might try one of those.

  • SleepSeen in studies
    In 22 trials with 1,806 adults, people who exercised for at least two months reported better sleep than people who did not. The gain was mostly in how they felt about their sleep. Measured sleep changed little, and the trials were at high risk of bias.
  • A safe placeSeen in studies
    In 15 trials with 980 adults, exercising in green places lifted mental health by a moderate amount. The review did not directly compare it with exercise indoors, and the trials were short.
  • Rhythm and lightThin evidence
    In 5 small studies that tracked movement, most found no clear difference in daily timing between people with and without depression. A few found less movement in the morning, or more late at night, in people with depression. They cannot say which comes first.
