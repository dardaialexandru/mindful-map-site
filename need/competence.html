Mind need

Competence

Feeling capable.

Some evidence · a link, cause not shown

In plain words

Competence is the feeling of being able to do things and to get better at them. It grows from small steps that work. People who were supported to feel capable reported feeling more capable, and the feeling goes with better well-being.

A simple way to picture it. The research sections below say how sure we are.

What research says

People who are supported to feel capable report feeling more capable. In a review of 881 groups (443,556 people), that support went with feeling competent at a moderately strong level, and feeling competent goes with better well-being.

The link between support for competence and doing better at tasks was small.

How far to trust itMost of the studies are links measured at one point in time, and the results vary a lot between studies. About four in ten of the averages could be inflated by unpublished studies. This review does not show that feeling capable causes better mood.

What you can try

  • Choose something you can get better at, and notice small progress.
  • Ask for feedback that tells you what you did well.

In everyday life

Made-up examples to help you see yourself. They are not study results.

Lia, 25 started learning guitar and can play two chords. Noticing that last week she could not is the point of the page.
Victor, 53 retrained in a new field and feels like a beginner again. Asking for feedback that says what he did well helps him keep going.
Hana, 40 is great at her job but hides it at home. Naming what she does well is an easy start.
