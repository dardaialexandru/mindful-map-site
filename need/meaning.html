Mind need

Meaning

Feeling that life has a point.

Some evidence · a link, cause not shown

In plain words

Meaning is the feeling that what you do matters, to you or to someone else. It does not have to be big. Looking after a child, a garden or a job done well all count. People with a stronger sense of purpose reported less stress, though the cause may run both ways.

A simple way to picture it. The research sections below say how sure we are.

What research says

People with a stronger sense of purpose report less stress. Across 16 groups and 108,391 people, the link was small to medium, and it showed up in 13 of the 16.

In one study of people over 50 (6,985 people followed for about four years), those with the strongest sense of purpose lived longer than those with the weakest.

A large pooling of 278 studies with over 250,000 people found that more meaning in life went with less depression, by a moderate amount. Life making sense to you showed the strongest link. Most studies looked at one moment in time, so they cannot show that meaning protects, and low mood can also make meaning harder to find.

How far to trust itThese are links only, and the cause may run the other way: people who feel well may find purpose more easily. Among people without chronic illness the second link was not clear. We found no proof that finding a purpose makes you live longer.

What you can try

  • Ask yourself what matters to you. A sense of purpose goes with less stress.
  • If life feels pointless for weeks, talk to a doctor or a counsellor.

In everyday life

Made-up examples to help you see yourself. They are not study results.

Rosa, 48 feels her job is pointless but loves running the Saturday club for children. Her sense of purpose lives there.
Ivan, 70 retired and felt adrift. He now teaches his grandson chess and says Tuesdays have a point again.
Chloe, 26 asks herself what she would miss if it were gone. Her answers (her friends, her dog, her paintings) tell her where her purpose lives.
