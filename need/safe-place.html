Body need

A safe place

Clean air, quiet, green space, somewhere you can rest.

Some evidence · mostly observation

In plain words

A safe place is where your body can switch off a little: quiet enough to sleep, air you can breathe, somewhere green to walk, a door you can close. The studies observe people, so they show links, not proof. But these are things most of us can feel.

A simple way to picture it. The research sections below say how sure we are.

What research says

A quieter place goes with better sleep and a calmer body. The World Health Organization advises under 53 decibels by day and under 45 at night for road noise.

People who live with more green space tend to have better mood: about 4% better odds for every 10% more green space, in 18 studies with over 3 million people. For anxiety there was no clear effect.

Exercise outdoors beat doing nothing by a large amount. It did not beat exercise indoors.

Air matters too. In a review of studies from 16 countries, cleaner air went with better mood: every 10 micrograms per cubic metre fewer fine particles went with about 10% better odds of a good mood (5 studies pooled). The authors say a cause is not confirmed.

How far to trust itThe noise review is narrative and partly based on animal studies, and one author works with an anti-noise group. The green space and air studies only observe people, cover few countries, and show a link, not a proven cause.

What you can try

  • A quieter bedroom makes sense, especially at night.
  • Spend time in green places, and exercise outside if you enjoy it.
  • Where you can choose, a place with cleaner air is a sensible direction. We do not yet know that cleaning the air improves mood.

In everyday life

Made-up examples to help you see yourself. They are not study results.

Nina, 30 lives on a busy road. Moving her bed to the back room made the nights quieter. The WHO suggests under 45 decibels at night for road noise.
Omar, 49 walks through a small park on his way to work. It adds ten minutes, and he arrives calmer. Living near more green space went with better mood in the studies.
Julia, 35 has a tiny flat with one chair by the window. That chair is her place to rest, and a safe place can be that small.

How it connects

Dashed line: a link seen in studies that only observe. Dash-dot line: thin evidence, or ideas that belong to the same framework. Dotted line: noted on the map, not yet researched here. Gold: feelings.

  • SleepSeen in studies
    A quieter place goes with better sleep.
  • MovementSeen in studies
    In 15 trials with 980 adults, exercising in green places lifted mental health by a moderate amount. The review did not directly compare it with exercise indoors, and the trials were short.
  • StabilitySeen in studies
    In a review of 47 studies, the threat of losing a home went with worse mental well-being in most studies that measured it. Most of the studies came from English-speaking countries.
