Mind need

Stability

Money, work and a place to live.

Solid for work and hours, some evidence for money and home

In plain words

Stability means the ground under your feet: a way to earn, a home that feels safe, enough money for the month. When these wobble, the mind tends to spend its energy on them. The studies show that work, home and money go with how people feel.

A simple way to picture it. The research sections below say how sure we are.

What research says

Having work matters for feeling well. A review of 324 studies found that people in work have better mood and fewer worries than people without it, and about 34% of unemployed people had psychological strain against 16% of employed people. Studies that followed people over time support work as a cause. Programmes for long-term job seekers lifted mood by a moderate amount.

Working 35 to 40 hours a week goes with better heart health than working 55 hours or more (22 and 37 studies, over 760,000 people).

Money seems to keep helping. In a US study of 33,391 workers, day-to-day well-being kept rising with income, with a levelling off only among the unhappiest group. Another analysis finds a levelling off near $200,000, so the ceiling is disputed.

A secure home goes with a calmer mind. Across 47 studies, people who felt their home was under threat reported more worry and distress. Money worries also weigh on mood in a review of 66 articles, partly through feeling out of control.

How far to trust itThe studies on money, home and debt show links, not proven causes, and most come from English-speaking countries. We found no figures for the debt review. The hours figures are estimates from models, and we do not know how they handled other factors.

What you can try

  • Weeks of 55 hours or more are worth cutting back if you can.
  • If you are out of work, structured programmes for job seekers have been shown to lift mood.
  • If money, work or home worries stay with you for weeks and your mood is low, talk to a doctor.

In everyday life

Made-up examples to help you see yourself. They are not study results.

Radu, 43 works 60 hours a week to keep up with rent. He is tired and short-tempered. The page notes that weeks of 55 hours or more are worth cutting back if possible.
Emma, 35 lost her job in spring and found the structured job-seeker programme useful. In the studies such programmes lifted mood by a moderate amount.
Hugo, 58 is anxious every time a letter from the landlord arrives. Feeling that your home is under threat went with more worry in the studies.
