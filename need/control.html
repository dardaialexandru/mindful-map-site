MIND NEED

Control

Being able to decide about your own life.

Some evidenceVideo · 1 min5 min read

The short answer

Feeling that you decide, even about small things, goes with better mood.

Some evidenceA link, cause not shown

Try this today

Look for one small choice that is yours, and make it on purpose.

In plain words

You are the one who decides, at least about some things.

Control here means feeling that you are the one who decides, at least about some things. It does not mean controlling everything. Even in a hard period there are usually a few small choices that are yours, and noticing them is the idea.

A simple way to picture it. The research below says how sure we are.

3

What research says

Links across many studies. Most cannot show what causes what.
How to read this
  • The grey bar is the full scale of link strength, from none (0) to complete (1).
  • The pale blue stretch is where the single studies fell. The dot is the average of 16 studies pooled (27,685 people in the review).
  • The coloured dots count the 20 studies by quality.

Source: Msetfi, Kornbrot and Halbrook 2024.

A stronger sense of control goes with better mood. In a review of 16 studies from the COVID years, the link was moderate. Feeling that you are the one who decides was more tied to feeling well than feeling that outside forces are in charge.

A large review of 881 groups of people (443,556 in all) found that when people are supported to make their own choices, they feel more autonomy, and they report better mood and more satisfaction with life. These are links across many studies; most do not show what causes what.

How far to trust itThe control review covers only the pandemic years and English-language studies. The larger review pools links, and its results differ a lot from study to study. We cannot say that raising control lifts mood.
4

What you can try

Small steps. Ideas, not tested treatments.

Notice which parts of your life you can decide and which you cannot. This is an idea, not a tested treatment.

Look for small choices that are yours today.

How it connects

How to read this

Thin evidenceSame frameworkFeeling this need may sit behind

Each line joins this need to another. The label on it says how well the link is backed by research, and how many studies the page text names. Tap a name to open it. A link seen in studies is not proof of cause.

Dashed line: a link seen in studies that only observe. Dash-dot line: thin evidence, or ideas that belong to the same framework. Dotted line: noted on the map, not yet researched here. Gold: feelings.

  • StabilityThin evidence
    In one study of 384 people who had just lost their jobs, those back in work six months later felt more in charge of their lives than those still without. One study only, and it cannot show which came first.
  • MeaningThin evidence
    In three small US studies, feeling capable, feeling that you decide, and feeling close to others each went with a stronger sense that life has meaning. They rely on questionnaires and cannot show cause.
  • CompetenceSame framework
    Control, competence and belonging are the three basic needs studied together in the large review of 881 groups of people.
  • Feeling: Stuck
    This need may sit behind this feeling.

Control also connects to stability: money, work and housing change how much you can choose.

Sources
  • Frontiers in Psychiatry 2024: sense of control and mood (16 studies).
  • Slemp et al. 2024: interpersonal support for basic psychological needs, well-being and performance (881 samples).
  • Houssemand et al. 2019, Frontiers in Psychology: perceived control in unemployment (384 newly unemployed people).
  • Martela, Ryan & Steger 2018, Journal of Happiness Studies: autonomy, competence, relatedness and meaning (3 studies).
