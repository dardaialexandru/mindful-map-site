Background

Things that shape you before you choose anything.

  1. Heredity

    Genes explain about a third of the differences in wellbeing. The rest comes from environment. Genes load the dice; they do not decide alone.

    Observed link only · Good evidence

  2. Childhood

    A hard childhood raises the risk of depression in adulthood. This comes from memory and observation, not from experiments. The idea that trauma is “written in your DNA” has not held up.

    Observed link only · Good evidence

  3. Surroundings

    Aircraft noise and pollution are linked with more depression.

    Observed link only · Limited evidence

  4. Personality

    People who are more organised and conscientious live somewhat longer, on average. It is an observed link.

    Observed link only · Medium evidence

Life stages and hormones

  1. All adults

    Daily hormone rhythms, sleep, stress and body weight matter at every age.

  2. Women

    The cycle (mild changes for most, serious symptoms for some), pregnancy and the months after birth (the biggest risk is an earlier history of depression), perimenopause (higher risk of depression), and after menopause (unclear).

  3. Men

    Testosterone follows a daily rhythm, and short sleep lowers it. With age, the drop is mostly explained by weight and illness. There is no monthly cycle.

  4. Older adults

    Almost half of dementia cases could be delayed or prevented by reducing 14 factors, such as high blood pressure, smoking, lack of exercise, hearing loss and social isolation. The estimate assumes each link is largely causal, which is not certain, and it guarantees nothing for any one person.

Everything here is general information from published research. It does not replace a doctor or therapist. Effects in this research are usually small or medium. Nothing here is a cure.
