Life around you

The setting your needs live in. Same rule: we say how sure we are.

  1. Relationships

    Warm, varied ties go with better health, but this is an observed link, not proof of cause. Couple therapy helps unhappy couples; short courses give small to moderate gains. For loneliness, face-to-face groups help in the short term. About two in three people who divorce show no lasting depression; roughly one in ten develops new depression and needs support.

    Observed link only · Medium evidence

  2. Work

    Burnout is a work-related state, not a medical diagnosis, and its symptoms can look like those of depression. Changing the work and building skills each give small gains, with weak evidence. Unemployment goes with worse mental health. Working 55 or more hours a week goes with a higher risk of stroke and, to a smaller degree, heart disease. Working from home depends on choice, amount and support. Job insecurity goes with worse mental health in long-term studies. Shift work goes with more heart attacks and diabetes, in observational studies only. Mindfulness at work helped stress, anxiety and sleep in trials; its effect on burnout is unclear.

    Observed link only · Medium evidence

  3. Habits

    A new habit takes months, not three weeks. Tracking your progress and “if this, then that” plans help by a small to medium amount. Plan for the days you miss.

    Shown to help in trials · Medium evidence

  4. Trauma

    Hard events are very common. PTSD is much rarer. Many people recover, and a notable share of cases ease within months. UK guidelines recommend trauma-focused therapy (CBT or EMDR) with a specialist for adults with PTSD. They advise against one-off “debriefing” right after an event, because trials found no benefit and one found a higher risk of PTSD. This site does not give therapy steps.

    Shown to help in trials · Good evidence

  5. Screens and social media

    The links with wellbeing are small and uneven, and most research is on teenagers. In adults, trials of cutting back on social media give conflicting results: one review found a small benefit, another found none. Breaks may need to last about a week or more to help. The US Surgeon General’s 2023 advisory is about young people and says the evidence is not yet enough to judge safety.

    Shown to help in trials · Limited evidence

  6. Racing thoughts and ADHD

    Racing or looping thoughts are not on the official list of ADHD symptoms. They can come from anxiety, low mood, stress, poor sleep or ADHD, so an assessment has to rule the others out. In adults, ADHD needs at least five symptoms, several of which started before age 12, and a specialist diagnosis. UK guidance recommends medication for some people and a structured psychological programme, which may include CBT.

    Observed link only · Medium evidence

  7. Long-lasting pain

    Exercise and CBT-based talking therapy help some people. Exercise gives small to moderate gains in moving and often eases pain a little, but trials were small, so we are not very sure. CBT changes pain itself very little, but gives small, reliable gains in distress and coping. UK guidance for long-lasting pain with no clear cause, read through summaries, recommends supervised group exercise and advises against starting several common painkillers, including opioids. Do not stop a medicine without talking to your doctor. About half of people with depression also report long-lasting pain, and the link runs both ways.

    Shown to help in trials · Medium evidence

  8. Long-term illness and mood

    Low mood and anxiety are common with long-term illness such as cancer, diabetes, heart disease and long COVID, and they can be treated. How common varies a lot between studies. In one trial of people with diabetes or heart disease, organised team care (doctor, nurse and follow-up) eased depression modestly. Tell your doctor if your mood changes.

    Observed link only · Limited evidence

  9. Autism in adults

    Many autistic adults have never been diagnosed, especially older adults. Hiding autistic traits to fit in goes with worse mental health. Anxiety and depression are common. UK guidance recommends assessment for adults, and supports such as group social learning, supported employment and CBT adapted to be concrete and structured. Autism is not an illness.

    Observed link only · Limited evidence

  10. Grief, alcohol and meaning

    Grief is normal. For a minority, roughly one in ten or fewer, it becomes a long-lasting illness. Drinking more goes with a higher risk of depression, and some studies suggest it may contribute, but the cause is uncertain. Alcohol also worsens sleep and mood.

    Observed link only · Medium evidence
